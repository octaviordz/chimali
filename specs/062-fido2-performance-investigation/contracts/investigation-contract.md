## Attribution and diagnostic decision

For each valid lookup interval, partition elapsed time into non-overlapping `running`, `runnable`, `coroutine-suspended`, `thread-sleeping`, `thread-uninterruptible-sleep`, and `unknown` intervals using linked coroutine lifecycle events and scheduler events on each actual dispatcher thread. A suspended coroutine is not attributed to the last thread's scheduling state. Retain raw event endpoints, thread transitions, partition totals and trace-loss diagnostics; all six buckets must sum to the lookup interval. Missing coroutine lifecycle, scheduler coverage, compatible clock data, or any unassignable interval is `unknown`, never zero or assumed CPU work. Await time is recorded as suspension; do not attribute its cause to storage, scheduling or a specific component without separate evidence.

The coroutine context element may be inherited by nested work, so operation-linked execution-segment intervals can overlap across dispatcher threads. Sweep their raw endpoints and scheduler transitions over wall time; maintain the set of active segment cookies and thread IDs, and union time rather than summing overlapping segment durations. For each non-overlapping wall-time slice, classify `running` when any active operation-linked thread is scheduled, otherwise `runnable-but-not-running` when any active operation-linked thread is runnable, otherwise `coroutine-suspended` when no operation-linked segment is active and the lookup is still open. Classify a remaining slice as `thread-sleeping` only when operation-linked segments are active, every active operation-linked thread has exactly one covering scheduler state `S`, no such thread is scheduled or runnable, and trace coverage is complete without conflicting endpoints or loss affecting the slice. Classify a remaining slice as `thread-uninterruptible-sleep` only when the same evidence conditions hold and every active thread has state `D`. These labels record only observed OS thread states; neither identifies a wait cause or implies coroutine suspension. A slice with mixed `S`/`D`, any other active-thread state, missing/conflicting endpoint, ambiguous combination, or trace loss is `unknown`. Require all six wall-time buckets to sum exactly to the lookup interval; conclusive disposition still requires residual `unknown` duration zero. Never interpret summed CPU execution time as wall time. This rule is required by the overlapping segments observed in the synthetic Android dispatcher-handoff trace.

Before baseline collection, characterize clock alignment, endpoint granularity and instrumentation overhead empirically on the designated device and build. Execute the approved [calibration protocol](../calibration-protocol.md), retain every independent session and trace-health result, and freeze the validated allowance `u` in nanoseconds before sampling. The 99% coverage claim at 95% familywise confidence is limited to the per-session clock/control observations under the two declared calibration conditions and the stated identity; it is not a guaranteed maximum error or a claim about population latency. Failed, unavailable or mismatched calibration leaves the investigation inconclusive. For raw duration `d`, retain sensitivity intervals `lower = max(0, d - k*u)` and `upper = d + k*u` at both `k=1` and `k=2`. The second scale is a predeclared engineering stress test; never subtract overhead from the raw duration.

Require ten eligible intervals in each group and compute raw median and maximum. For the 20 ms diagnostic rule, calculate the median of the ten lower bounds and the maximum of the ten upper bounds in each group. A group is `material` if its lower-bound median is ≥20 ms; `not-material` if its maximum upper bound is <20 ms; otherwise it is `inconclusive`. Overall precedence: if either group is incomplete or inconclusive, the overall result is inconclusive; otherwise it is material if either group is material, and not-material if both are not-material. Apply these group and overall rules at both allowance scales. A conclusive result requires identical determinate overall classifications at both scales; otherwise it is inconclusive. A lower-bound median exactly equal to 20 ms is material; an upper bound exactly equal to 20 ms is not a not-material result. Keep all attempts and distinguish the calibration coverage claim from any latency-population claim. This threshold is a diagnostic trigger, not a physical-device acceptance criterion or proof of the historical cause.

For a conclusive current-workload disposition, each eligible interval also needs a complete six-bucket partition with residual unknown duration zero; otherwise leave the investigation open. Report running, runnable, suspended, thread-sleeping, and thread-uninterruptible-sleep durations per interval without subtracting them from the elapsed threshold. A material elapsed classification identifies an interval for component-level attribution, not proof that any particular component caused the cost: profile its component and suspension/blocking causes before choosing an optimization. A not-material disposition requires both groups to meet the upper-bound rule. An individual interval's bounds may cross 20 ms without invalidating a material group when that group's lower-bound median is still ≥20 ms; the group is inconclusive only when neither group-level rule decides it. If either group is inconclusive, its partition is incomplete, calibration or sensitivity validation fails, or correlation or evidence validity fails, report overall `inconclusive` and collect additional predeclared evidence; never infer that the historical 134.339 ms gap was caused by the lookup.

If material, measure components before selecting a change and use a comparable before/after workload with same device, build configuration, state, collection rules and analysis; disclose any change preventing comparison. If not-material, record a no-change decision on this lead. Inconclusive or missing absolute data remains explicitly inconclusive. Under the owner-approved 2026-11-02 amendment, the completed T017 before/after comparison may receive a bounded comparative disposition when the ten eligible before and after warm samples have disjoint sensitivity-expanded median intervals at both scales. Report only a sampled median difference; make no population-latency or causal claim, and do not relabel the absolute 20 ms result as material or not-material. Transfer the still-material cold lead to an accessible separate follow-up record before closing this investigation. The warm-registration reliability issue remains separate. Any future expanded measurement policy and its decision rule must be declared before collection and retained with the original data.
